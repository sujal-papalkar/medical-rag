## Dear Vaidyas,

<!-- PAGE: 2 -->
In today's era, due to the newly developing set of Hetu (causative factors) and complexity of existing Hetu, it is getting difficult for a physician to extrapolate the exact Samprapti and Avastha of vyadhi. Also, it is very difficult to identify the stage of a disease as patient comes from different backgrounds e.g., patient visiting ayurvedic physician with some disease while consuming modern medicines. Generally, it is observed that while managing the bio chemical parameters for that specific disease, there is considerable loss of quality of life of a patient. There is a need for better clinically tested alternatives from Ayurved. Here Ayurveda's fundamentals come in picture, that it treats the patient along with maintaining or improving quality of life of a patient.

Since there is ever-growing complexity in causative factors, there is development of new diseases, with complex criss cross pathologies, it is difficult to diagnose the patient under one specific disease. Also, there are new group of symptoms and diagnostic methods coming in picture. Because of this, it becomes very difficult for an Ayurved practitioner to give enough time, efforts, limited mental bandwidth to study Ayurved first and then analyse newly coming diseases and diagnostic methods, which are not discussed under clear heads in classical texts of Ayurved. Most of such diseases are considered under Gudhalinga Vyadhi. Additionally, with increased challenges of today's era, it is becoming difficult for every Vaidya to understand the text at base level, with the help of grammar, Teekas, Nyayas etc. Hence it is very challenging to identify the exact stage of a vyadhi, along with its Sadhyasadhyatva, dosha dhatu Bala, satva etc. This leads to higher risk of misdiagnosis of the condition of a patient or wrong judgement about the chikitsa Tatva and chikitsa dravya and so on.

Considering DM, India has become the global capital with increasing number of diagnosed and undiagnosed or lately diagnosed patients. Currently management of blood sugar level is considered as the only method of treatment. But as all of us know that management of BSL only is not enough treatment for DM. Even patients with controlled BSL develop further DM complications over the period. Ayurved has its own terminology and way of explaining diseases. Many of the times DM is closely correlated with Prameha in Ayurveda. Many of the times Vaidya's get confused about the Purvaroopa, Rupa, Upadrava or even with Vyadhisankara when it comes to patients visiting ayurvedic physicians with known diagnosis of DM. Hence it is very difficult to understand the Samprapti of Meha resembling disease or DM with involvement of different Srotasa or Avayava. For the same reason it becomes challenging to understand the illness and its complications. Hence there is gap between understanding of diabetes patient and may be its possible relation with Prameha.

One has to understand in clinical settings that these well diagnosed patients by modern medicine when visit ayurvedic clinic and ayurvedic diagnosis starts with Hetu, Lakshane, Samprapti, sometimes already prevalent complications of DM. One of the most important factors a Vaidya has to consider is modern medicines the patient is consuming. All these medicines are likely to be uptarpanottha in nature which equally interferes with Samprapti. This is one of the most important aspects while designing Vyadhipratyanik chikitsa in Ayurveda. As we all know in such patients almost all dhatus, doshas and malas
